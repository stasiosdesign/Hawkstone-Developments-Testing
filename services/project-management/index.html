The gap this fills

Where projects lose their thread

Once construction begins, the pace of the project changes. Decisions get made on site, questions arrive by phone, and drawings that took months to develop are suddenly being interpreted by people who were not part of the conversation.

The concerns clients raise at this stage are consistent. Communication becomes patchy. Timescales drift. Choices are made without them. They are unsure whether what the contractor is asking for is reasonable, who should be coordinating information between the different consultants, and whether the finished building will actually match the approved design.

None of that means the contractor is doing anything wrong. It usually means nobody is holding the design side of the project while the building side takes over.

What this service is, and what it is not

This is a defined professional appointment rather than an open-ended promise to run your site. Being exact about it matters, because the two roles are routinely confused.

What we do

  • Review the approved design and technical information before work begins
  • Chair a pre-start meeting with you and your contractor
  • Attend agreed site inspections and record what we find
  • Answer the contractor’s design questions as they arise
  • Coordinate with specialist consultants and Building Control where required
  • Inspect the substantially completed work and prepare a snagging schedule

What we do not do

  • We do not supervise the site continuously. Inspections are periodic and agreed in advance
  • We do not act as construction manager or take over any part of the contractor’s role
  • We are not responsible for site safety
  • We are not responsible for the contractor’s workmanship or construction methods
  • We do not direct the trades or manage the build programme

Your contractor remains responsible for carrying out the work, supervising the trades, maintaining site safety and delivering the project in accordance with the contract and statutory requirements. Our role is to give you an informed point of contact and an independent record of what is happening on your project.

Scope

How the appointment works

The number of visits and the exact level of support are agreed in your individual proposal. A typical appointment includes up to two site inspections each calendar month.

Before work starts

  • Pre-construction review We review the approved design, the technical information, consultant requirements and any planning or Building Control matters that affect the construction stage.

  • Pre-start meeting We meet you and the contractor to go through the proposed works, the programme, communication arrangements and the key milestones. A written agenda is prepared and issued.

During construction

  • Periodic site inspections Agreed visits to review general progress, visible workmanship and apparent conformity with the approved design information.

  • Written site reports After each visit you receive a report recording progress, our observations, recommended actions and the work expected over the following weeks.

  • Construction-stage communication Telephone and email support throughout the build, and answers to design-related questions as they come up.

  • Design and consultant coordination Liaison with the contractor, specialist consultants and Building Control where reasonably required, to keep project information aligned and respond to issues raised on site.

Towards completion

  • Pre-completion inspection A review of the substantially completed work, and a schedule of visible defects, incomplete items and outstanding work for the contractor to address.

  • Completion support We review the status of the recorded items and support communication between you and the contractor as the project moves towards handover, where this is included in your appointment.

When construction-stage support earns its place

Not every project needs it. It matters most where the detail is the point of the project, or where you cannot be on site often enough to catch things as they happen.

Bespoke and architect-designed homes

Where materials, junctions and finishes were chosen deliberately, substitutions made quickly on site can quietly undo the design.

Luxury architecture →

Rural and barn conversions

Existing structures reveal themselves as work progresses, and decisions often have to be tested against what the approval actually permits.

Rural conversions →

Heritage and listed properties

Historic fabric, specified materials and the conditions attached to consent all have to be respected as the work is carried out.

Heritage projects →

This is likely to suit you if

  • You have planning permission and technical drawings, and construction is about to begin
  • You will not be on site regularly, or would rather not be the person interpreting the drawings
  • You want a written record of progress rather than verbal updates
  • Your project involves details, materials or planning conditions that need to be followed closely

It is probably not what you need if

  • You want someone to run the site day to day and manage the trades. That is your contractor’s role
  • You are still at design or planning stage, in which case an early conversation is the better starting point
  • Your build is straightforward, your contractor is well known to you, and you can be there regularly

Common questions

What does a residential construction project manager do?

In our case, it means holding the design side of a project while it is being built. We review the approved information before work starts, chair a pre-start meeting, inspect the work periodically, report on what we find, coordinate with consultants and Building Control, and inspect the finished work against the approved design.

What is the difference between a project manager and a site manager?

A site manager works for the contractor and runs the site day to day: sequencing the trades, ordering materials, maintaining safety and keeping the build moving. We work for you, and independently of the contractor. We are not on site every day and we do not direct the work. We check that what is being built matches what was approved, and we tell you what we see.

When should you become involved?

Before the contractor starts, ideally. The pre-construction review and pre-start meeting are the two points where this appointment does the most good, because they set out how the project will run while everything is still open.

How often will you inspect the work?

The frequency is agreed in your proposal and is set against the length and complexity of the build. A typical appointment includes up to two inspections a calendar month.

Will I receive a report after every site visit?

Yes. Each visit is followed by a written report recording progress, our observations, any recommended actions, and the work expected over the following weeks.

Can you check whether the contractor is following the approved design?

That is the core of the service. Our inspections review general progress, visible workmanship and apparent conformity with the approved design information, and anything that departs from it is recorded in the report and raised with you and the contractor.

Do you coordinate with consultants and Building Control?

Yes, where it is reasonably required. That includes liaising with the contractor, structural engineers and other specialist consultants, and with Building Control, to keep project information aligned and to respond to issues raised on site.

Do you carry out pre-completion and snagging inspections?

Yes. As the work approaches completion we inspect the substantially completed building and prepare a schedule of visible defects, incomplete items and outstanding work for the contractor to address. Where it is included in the appointment, we then review the status of those items as the project moves to handover.

How much does construction-stage project management cost?

Fees depend on the length of the construction programme, the number of inspections and the level of coordination involved, so they are confirmed in your individual proposal rather than quoted as a fixed figure. Tell us what the project is and when it is due to start, and we will set it out.

Talk to us before work starts on site

The most useful time to bring us in is before your contractor begins, while the pre-construction review and the pre-start meeting can still shape how the project runs. Tell us what the project is, where it has reached and when it is due to start on site.
