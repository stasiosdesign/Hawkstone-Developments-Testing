Hawkstone is an architectural design and planning practice for homes. As luxury residential architects working across Derbyshire, Staffordshire and Cheshire, we take individual houses from the first conversation through concept, planning and technical design, with construction-stage support where you want a single point of accountability through to completion.

New build house architects: bespoke homes on your own plot

A new home on a plot you own, or one you are about to buy, is the purest version of this work. Nothing is inherited; the site, the brief and the budget set the terms.

We design bespoke new build homes for private clients and self-build plots, including custom build sites where the plot comes with a design code to work within. The first question is always planning: what the site allows, what the local authority's housing and design policies expect, and whether the plot sits in the Green Belt or open countryside, where a new house has to make a specific case. Where a rural site qualifies, Paragraph 84 of the NPPF allows an isolated new home of exceptional design quality, and we have worked that route.

The second question is what kind of house. Contemporary, traditional or a considered blend of both: we don't carry a house style from one project to the next, and the right answer usually comes from the site.

Replacement dwellings and knock-down rebuilds

Sometimes the best plot is the one under a house you already own. If the existing property is poorly built, badly arranged or simply the wrong house for the site, replacing it can be more sensible than repairing it, and it avoids the compromises of working around an old structure.

Replacement dwelling policy varies by authority and is most restrictive in the Green Belt, where the new house is generally expected not to be materially larger than the one it replaces. That constraint is negotiable in practice more often than the policy wording suggests, but only with a design-led case. We establish what is realistically achievable before you commit to the idea.

Complete redesigns and substantial extensions of existing homes

Not every transformation starts with demolition. Many of our clients own a house with good bones and the wrong plan: a period farmhouse, a mid-century bungalow, a large family home that no longer works for the family in it.

This is the redesign of a whole house rather than a single extension. It usually means reorganising the plan, opening the house to its garden and light, adding significant new space, and bringing the fabric and services up to the standard of a new home. Sandboro House Farm and Furs House are both this kind of project.

Luxury residential architecture, as we practise it

“Luxury” is an easy word to use and a hard one to deliver. For us it does not mean a specification list. It means a house that responds to its site and its orientation, that is generous with light and volume where it matters, that uses a restrained palette of materials chosen to last, and that is detailed with the same care inside and out.

It also means proportion and restraint. The high-end residential architects whose work lasts are the ones who know what to leave out. We would rather design a house that still looks right in thirty years than one that photographs well next spring.

Every project is modelled in three dimensions and, where useful, visualised, so that you are making decisions about spaces rather than lines on a plan.

Budget and planning certainty before you commit

The two things clients worry about most are whether their aspirations fit their budget, and whether the scheme will get planning permission. We deal with both before design starts, not after.

  • Feasibility first. Every project begins with a site appraisal: the property or plot, the planning constraints that apply to it, and the opportunities we can see. That happens before any detailed design work, so you are not paying for drawings of something that cannot be built or consented.

  • A budget you can hold. We establish the budget at the brief stage and design to it, with decision points built into the process where cost is reviewed against the developing design. The cost of a bespoke home is driven by size, specification and the site; we give honest pointers early and involve a cost consultant where the project warrants one.

  • Planning strategy, not just an application. For a new home, a replacement dwelling or a large extension in the countryside, the planning case is part of the design. We shape the scheme around the policy it has to satisfy and bring in a planning consultant where the case needs one.

A desktop review of your site beforehand, an hour with a lead designer, and a written report with the likely planning route, the risks we can see, and next steps.

How a bespoke home comes together

  1. Feasibility consultation. A fixed-fee, 60-minute session with a lead designer, with a desktop review of your site beforehand and a written report afterwards.

  2. Brief and surveys. We agree the aspirations, priorities, budget and architectural character, arrange the topographical and building surveys, and bring in any specialists the project needs: planning, landscape, interiors, ecology.

  3. Concept design. Layout, scale, appearance and the overall direction of the house, developed through drawings and 3D modelling so the design can be judged as spaces rather than plans.

  4. Planning and design development. Materials and key details refined with you, and the planning application prepared, coordinated and managed through to decision.

  5. Technical design. Construction drawings, specifications and coordination with structural engineers, energy consultants and other specialists, so the house can be priced and built accurately.

  6. Construction support. Contractor selection, design queries answered on site, and oversight through the build where you appoint us for it.

You can appoint us to take the project to planning approval, or for the whole journey from first sketch to handover.

Questions we're asked about bespoke and luxury homes

How much does it cost to design a luxury home?

Architectural fees are usually a percentage of construction cost or a fixed fee agreed per stage, and they scale with the complexity of the house rather than its label. The construction cost itself is driven by size, specification and site conditions. We give budget pointers at the feasibility stage and design to an agreed figure from the brief onwards.

What is included in a luxury residential architectural design service?

Feasibility advice, measured surveys, concept and developed design, 3D visualisation, planning submissions, technical drawings and specifications, and coordination of the consultants the project needs. Construction-stage support is available where you want it. Each appointment is tailored to the project.

Do I need planning permission to build a new home?

Yes. A new house always requires full planning permission, and a replacement dwelling does too. In the Green Belt and open countryside the policy tests are stricter, and the design has to make a specific case. We establish the likely route and its risks before you commit to design work.

How long does it take to design and build a bespoke home?

Realistically, design and planning take a matter of months, with a full planning application typically determined in eight to thirteen weeks once submitted. Technical design follows approval, and construction depends on the size of the house. We set out a programme at the feasibility stage.

Can you design a bespoke home around our budget?

Yes, and we would rather know the budget at the start. We establish it at the brief stage, design to it, and build cost reviews into the process so the figure is tested as the design develops rather than at tender.

Do you design both contemporary and traditional homes?

Yes. Our projects range from contemporary houses to traditional ones and to considered blends of the two. We don't impose a house style; the site, its context and your brief decide it.

Can you redesign or extend our existing house instead of replacing it?

Yes. Where a house has good bones, a complete redesign with substantial new space can be the better route, and it is a large part of our work. Where the existing house is the problem, replacement can be more sensible. A feasibility consultation is where we look at both.

Do you manage the project through construction?

Where you appoint us for it. We can assist with contractor selection, respond to design queries and oversee the work on site so that what gets built is what was designed. You can also appoint us for the design and planning stages alone.
