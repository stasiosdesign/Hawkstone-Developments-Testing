Hawkstone is an architectural design and planning practice for homes, working across Derbyshire, Staffordshire and Cheshire. We design homes for the people who will live in them. We don't build speculatively and we don't sell property.

What "luxury" means in our work

Not a style, and not a price bracket we advertise. It means a house designed around how you actually live, sited to make the most of its outlook and light, built from materials chosen to look better in twenty years than on handover, and detailed to a standard that survives contact with a builder.

It also means the design is resolved before construction starts. Most of the cost overruns we're brought in to fix trace back to decisions deferred rather than materials chosen.

We design both contemporary and traditional houses. The region's housing stock and its planning context call for both, and neither is a default.

The three kinds of project this covers

Bespoke new homes and self-build plots

You own a plot, or you're buying one. The work runs from testing what the site and local policy will support, through design and planning, to the technical information a contractor can build from. Plot owners often come to us before purchase, which is the right time — what a site will support is the single largest variable in the budget.

Replacement dwellings and knock-down-and-rebuild

You own a house that isn't worth keeping, on a plot that is. Replacement dwelling policy governs what you can put in its place — commonly framed around the scale of the existing building, and more tightly constrained in the Green Belt, where the comparison is stricter. Whether replacing or extensively remodelling is the better route is a question worth answering before you decide.

Complete redesigns and major extensions

You have a house with good bones in the right place, and you want it substantially transformed — reconfigured, extended, re-clad, reoriented to its garden and its light. Often the most cost-effective route to the house you want, and frequently the most interesting design problem.

If your house is listed or in a conservation area, the consent process is different — start on our Heritage Projects page.

Planning permission for a new home in the countryside

Much of our region is Green Belt or open countryside, where new houses are constrained rather than simply permitted. The routes that do exist are worth knowing before you buy a site:

  • Replacement dwellings, where an existing house can be replaced within limits on scale.
  • Infill and settlement-boundary plots, where local policy allows.
  • Paragraph 84 houses — the exceptional-design route for isolated homes in the countryside. Genuinely rare and demanding, but real.
  • Conversions of rural buildings, which is a different service: see Rural Conversions.

We assess which of these applies to a site at feasibility stage, before you commit.

From brief to finished house

  1. Feasibility consultation. A fixed-fee, 60-minute session with a lead designer, with a desktop review of your site beforehand and a written report afterwards: what the site is likely to support, the planning route, and the sensible next step.

  2. Brief and survey. How you want to live, what the budget needs to cover, and a measured and topographical survey to design from.

  3. Concept design. Options tested against the site, the brief and the budget. Decisions made here shape everything downstream.

  4. Planning. A developed scheme, the supporting documents, and an application we prepare and manage.

  5. Technical design. Building Regulations and construction drawings, specifications, and coordination of engineers and consultants — the information that lets contractors price accurately.

  6. Tender and build. Contractor selection, and oversight through construction where you want a single accountable point from start to finish.

Controlling cost. We set a budget at the outset and test the design against it at each stage, with cost consultant input where the project warrants it. The expensive mistakes in a bespoke house are structural and geometric — they're made early, on paper, and they're cheap to avoid there.

Who this service is for

It's for you if:

  • You own or are buying a plot in Derbyshire, Staffordshire or Cheshire and want a bespoke house designed for it.
  • You own a house you're considering replacing, and want to know what policy would allow in its place.
  • You want an existing house transformed substantially rather than tidied up.
  • You're self-building and want a practice to take the design and planning, and as much of the delivery as you want.
  • You're a landowner or small developer commissioning a small number of high-quality homes.

It probably isn't if:

  • You're looking to buy a finished house. We design homes for clients; we don't sell property.
  • Your project is a single-room extension or a loft conversion — worth a conversation, but not what this service is built around.
  • Your house is listed or in a conservation area: start with Heritage Projects.
  • Your building is a barn or agricultural building: start with Rural Conversions.

Questions we're asked about designing a new home

How much does it cost to design a bespoke or luxury home?

Design fees are normally a percentage of construction cost or a fixed fee against a defined scope, and they vary with how much of the journey you want us to handle — planning only, through technical design, or through construction. We set out a fee basis at the feasibility stage rather than after you've committed.

Do I need planning permission for a new home or a replacement dwelling?

Yes. A new house always needs full planning permission, and in the countryside and Green Belt the policy hurdles are significant. Replacement dwellings are generally governed by limits relative to the existing house. Which route applies to your site is what the feasibility stage establishes.

How long does it take to design and build a bespoke home?

Design and planning typically run several months to a year depending on complexity and how quickly decisions are made; construction commonly a year or more. Anyone quoting materially less is either working on something simpler or hasn't allowed for planning.

How do you control cost as the design develops?

By setting the budget as a design constraint from the start, testing the scheme against it at each stage, and bringing in cost consultant input where the project warrants it. Cost control is a design discipline, not a stage at the end.

Do you design contemporary and traditional homes?

Both. The right answer depends on the site, the setting and what you want to live in — and in a region with this much designated landscape and historic fabric, the planning context often has a view too.

Can you redesign or extend our existing house instead of building new?

Yes, and it's frequently the better option. Whether to replace or transform depends on the existing structure, the plot, planning policy and your budget. We'll give you a straight comparison rather than steering you toward the larger project.

Do you manage the project through construction?

We can. Some clients engage us for design and planning only; others want a single accountable point from first sketch to completion, including tendering and construction oversight.
